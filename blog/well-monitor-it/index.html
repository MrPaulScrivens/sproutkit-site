When the IEP Team Says "We'll Monitor It"

October 16, 2026

You asked for something specific. More speech minutes, a reading intervention, an adult nearby for the part of the day that keeps going wrong.

The answer was that the team would monitor it and revisit at the next annual review.

Everyone nodded and the meeting moved on. Now you are in the car trying to work out whether that was a yes.

What monitoring is

Watching a child closely for a term is a reasonable thing for a team to want, and the person who offers it almost always means it.

Monitoring also has no minutes attached to it, no provider assigned to it, and nothing written down for anyone to work from twelve months later.

So you asked for a service and got a schedule back. Whether that counts as a refusal matters. When a district refuses to change the services your child receives, it owes you written notice explaining what it refused, why, what it based that on, and what other options it considered. 34 CFR 300.503.

Ask what would end it

Arguing with monitoring tends to go in circles. Asking what would end it gets you an answer you can write down.

If we monitor for a year, what would have to show up in the data for the team to say monitoring wasn’t enough? I’d like to know the number now, so we’re all watching for the same thing.

That question moves the decision from a feeling somebody will have next June to a threshold somebody says out loud today, while you write it down.

If the answer is that they will know it when they see it, narrow it.

Which two things will you be looking at, and who is collecting them?

Then put a date on it, because a year is a long time to wait for the first look.

Can we set a check-in for January, with the data we just named?

If what is being monitored is tied to a goal on the IEP and the data shows it stalling, you can ask for a meeting before the annual review. The team has to revise the IEP, as appropriate, to address a lack of expected progress. 34 CFR 300.324(b)(1)(ii)(A).

What to send afterward

Send this within two days, while the room’s own words are still in your notes.

Subject: [child's name], monitoring plan

Dear [case manager's name],

Thank you for meeting on [date]. I asked for [the service,
with the amount you asked for]. The team decided to monitor
instead and revisit at [when].

My notes say the team will be watching [what you were told, as
close to word for word as you have it]. If I have that wrong,
please correct me in writing.

Could the monitoring plan be written into the IEP or sent to
me in writing? Specifically:

  - What data will be collected
  - Who collects it and how often
  - What result would mean a service is needed
  - When I will see the data

Since the team decided against adding [the service] for now,
I am also requesting prior written notice of that decision
under 34 CFR 300.503.

Thank you,
[your name]

A monitoring plan with a trigger written into it can be met or missed, and that is what makes it a plan.

Your state may add to this

This covers federal law. States add their own special education rules on top, and some set shorter timelines.

Check what your state requires before you quote a deadline to anyone.

What to do this week

If “we’ll monitor it” came up, send the email above within two days of the meeting.

Put a reminder in your own calendar for the halfway point, and ask for the data collected so far.

If nothing has been collected by then, you will have that in writing too.
