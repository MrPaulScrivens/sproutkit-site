What to Send After an IEP Meeting

October 16, 2026

The meeting is over. You have a page of notes, half of them in a shorthand you will not be able to read by Friday.

Everyone was pleasant. Things were agreed to out loud. Some of them made it into the document and some only made it into the conversation.

Send one email in the next two days and the conversation becomes a record.

Why it matters

The people in that room almost certainly meant what they said. People also change schools, change roles, and go on leave.

The IEP is what follows your child into next year’s classroom. Your email is the dated account of everything that was said around it.

What goes in it

Keep it short. Four parts.

What you understood was agreed. Services, minutes, start dates, and anyone who said they would follow up on something.

What was turned down, if anything, in the words that were used.

What is still open, and who said they would get back to you.

A request to correct anything you got wrong, in writing.

The email

Subject: [child's name], notes from the [date] IEP meeting

Dear [case manager's name],

Thank you for meeting with me on [date]. I want to make sure
I understood everything correctly, so here are my notes.

What we agreed:
  - [service, minutes, and start date]
  - [anything someone said they would do, and by when]

What the team decided against:
  - [what you asked for, and what you were told]

Still open:
  - [the question, and who is getting back to me]

If I have any of this wrong, please correct me in writing.

[Only if something was turned down] I am also requesting
prior written notice of the decision not to [what was
declined], under 34 CFR 300.503.

Could you also send me a copy of the final IEP when it is
ready?

Thank you,
[your name]

Two requests worth adding

If you left without a copy of the IEP, ask for one. The district has to give you a copy at no cost. 34 CFR 300.322(f).

If something you asked for was turned down, even politely, ask for prior written notice. When a district refuses to change your child’s services, it owes you a written explanation of what it refused, why, what it based that on, and what else it considered. 34 CFR 300.503.

When the reply comes

Keep every reply in the same email thread. A year from now, one thread with dates on it is far easier to work from than a folder of separate messages.

If nobody replies, you still have a dated record of what you understood, sent while the meeting was fresh.

Your state may add to this

This covers federal law. States add their own special education rules on top, and some set shorter timelines.

Check what your state requires before you quote a deadline to anyone.

What to do this week

Send the email within two days of the meeting.

File the reply, or the date you sent it and heard nothing, in the same folder as the IEP.
